A cultivator is a secondary tillage farm implement used to stir and pulverize the soil, prepare a smooth seedbed, and control weeds. What is a Cultivator?
Unlike a plough, which cuts deep and inverts the soil, a cultivator works near the surface to loosen dirt, aerate roots, and uproot weeds without turning the lower soil layers upside down. Main Types of Cultivators
• Spring-Tyne Cultivators: Fitted with flexible, spring-mounted tynes that bend upon hitting rocks, making them ideal for stony or uneven fields.
• Rigid-Tyne Cultivators: Built with fixed shanks that do not bend, providing the strength needed for hard, dry, or rocky soils.
• Duckfoot Cultivators: Equipped with wide, flat, duck-foot-shaped blades designed for shallow tillage and thorough weed cutting.
• Disc Cultivators: Uses concave discs to break large soil clumps and mix surface crop residue into the dirt.
• Row Crop Cultivators: Built specifically to maneuver precisely between growing crop rows for targeted weeding and aeration.
]
Key Uses and Benefits
• Weed Destruction: Uproots weeds and disrupts their photosynthesis by burying leaves.
• Soil Aeration: Breaks surface crusts to allow vital air, water, and nutrients to reach plant roots.
• Seedbed Preparation: Pulverizes soil clods left behind by primary ploughing to create a fine tilth for sowing.
• Moisture Retention: Helps preserve underlying soil moisture by sealing the top layer.
